9  Independence Test

9.1 Classical Theory

The Pearson correlation test and its robust extensions follow a three-tier hierarchy based on what is assumed about the joint distribution.

Assumption \(\kappa_{22}\) at \(H_0\colon\rho=0\) Null tested
Gaussian \(1\) (exact \(t_{n-2}\)) \(X \perp Y\)
Independent, non-Gaussian \(1\) (asymptotic) \(X \perp Y\)
Neither must estimate \(\rho = 0\)

Under Gaussianity, \(\rho = 0\) and \(X \perp Y\) are equivalent, so \(T = \sqrt{n-2}\,\hat\rho/\sqrt{1-\hat\rho^2}\) is an exact independence test. Without Gaussianity but under independence, \(\kappa_{22} = 1\) still holds at \(H_0: X \perp Y\), so \(\sqrt{n}\,\hat\rho \xrightarrow{d} N(0, 1)\). For the strictly weaker \(H_0: \rho = 0\) without assuming independence, \(\kappa_{22}\) must be estimated, giving asymptotic variance \[ v^2 = \frac{1}{n}\!\left(\omega_x^2 + \omega_y^2 + \kappa_{22} + \tfrac{5}{2}\right). \]

The ECE test follows the same hierarchy: Gaussian or independent errors give \(\kappa_{22} = 1\); otherwise \(\hat\kappa_{22}\) is estimated via the MoM construction below.

9.2 Estimators for \(\kappa_{22}\)

Three estimators are developed below.

Regression

With \(T_k^{(2)}(X,Y) = \sum_i (X_i - X_{i+k})^2(Y_i - Y_{i+k})^2\), \[\begin{align} \hat\kappa_{22}^{\mathrm{reg}} &= \frac{2T_1^{(2)}(X,Y) - T_2^{(2)}(X,Y)}{2n\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} - \frac{2\hat\sigma_{xy}^2}{\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} - 1 && \text{(general)} \\[4pt] &= \frac{2T_1^{(2)}(X,Y) - T_2^{(2)}(X,Y)}{2n\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} - 1 && (H_0) \end{align}\]

Matrix (\(\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat\sigma_{xy})\))

\[\begin{align} \hat\kappa_{22}^{V} &= \frac{n\,\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat\sigma_{xy})}{\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} - \frac{3\hat\sigma_{xy}^2 + 4\hat\sigma_{xy}\hat\omega_{xy}}{2\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} - \frac{\hat\omega_x^2}{\hat\sigma_x^2} - \frac{\hat\omega_y^2}{\hat\sigma_y^2} - \frac{5}{2} && \text{(general)} \\[4pt] &= \frac{n\,\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat\sigma_{xy})}{\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} - \frac{\hat\omega_x^2}{\hat\sigma_x^2} - \frac{\hat\omega_y^2}{\hat\sigma_y^2} - \frac{5}{2} && (H_0) \end{align}\]

Method of Moments

\[\begin{align} \hat\kappa_{22}^{\mathrm{MoM}} &= \frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_i S_{xx,i}\,S_{yy,i}}{\bar S_{xx}\,\bar S_{yy}} - \frac{3\hat\sigma_{xy}^2 + 4\hat\sigma_{xy}\hat\omega_{xy}}{\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} && \text{(general)} \\[4pt] &= \frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_i S_{xx,i}\,S_{yy,i}}{\bar S_{xx}\,\bar S_{yy}} && (H_0) \end{align}\]

9.3 Estimating \(\kappa_{22}\) via \(V\)

9.3.1 Estimating \(\kappa_{22}\) via \(V_{xy}\)

Let \(s_i^{(k)} = (X_i - X_{i+k})(Y_i - Y_{i+k})\) and \(S_i = \frac{s_i^{(1)} + s_{i+1}^{(1)} - s_i^{(2)}}{2}\) as in the Multiplier Bootstrap chapter, so that \[ \boxed{\bar{S} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n S_i = \frac{2T_1(X,Y) - T_2(X,Y)}{2n} = \hat\sigma_{xy}.} \] Since \(S_i\) depends only on indices \(i-2, i-1, i\), terms at lags \(\ge 3\) are independent, so \[ \operatorname{Var}(\bar{S}) = \frac{1}{n^2}\!\left[\sum_i \operatorname{Var}(S_i) + 2\sum_i \operatorname{Cov}(S_i, S_{i+1}) + 2\sum_i \operatorname{Cov}(S_i, S_{i+2})\right]. \] Replacing each population quantity with its sample analog gives the lag-2 variance estimate \[ V := \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\bar{S}) = \frac{1}{n^2}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n S_i^2 + \sum_{i=1}^n (S_i S_{i+1} + S_{i+1} S_i) + \sum_{i=1}^n (S_i S_{i+2} + S_{i+2} S_i)\right), \] or in matrix form, writing \(\mathbf{S} = (S_1, \ldots, S_n)^\top\), \[ \boxed{V = \frac{1}{n^2}\,\mathbf{S}^\top(I + C_1 + C_1^\top + C_2 + C_2^\top)\mathbf{S}.} \]

Since \(\bar S = (2T_1 - T_2)/(2n)\), we have \(V \approx \operatorname{Var}(2T_1 - T_2)/(4n^2)\). The variance on the right has a general closed form; under \(H_0\) (\(\sigma_{xy} = 0\)) it simplifies so we can solve for \(\kappa_{22}\) from \(V\).

Derivation: \(\operatorname{Var}(\bar{S})\) from \(\Sigma_Z\)

Since \(\bar{S} = (2T_1 - T_2)/(2n)\), \[ \operatorname{Var}(\bar{S}) = \frac{1}{4n^2}\bigl(4[\Sigma_Z]_{55} - 4[\Sigma_Z]_{56} + [\Sigma_Z]_{66}\bigr). \] The general \(\Sigma_Z\) entries from the Z-Test chapter are \[[\Sigma_Z]_{55} = 2n\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2(2\kappa_{22}+1) - 2n\sigma_{xy}^2 + 2n\sigma_y^2\omega_x^2 + 2n\sigma_x^2\omega_y^2 + 4n\sigma_{xy}\omega_{xy},\] \[[\Sigma_Z]_{56} = 4n(\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2\kappa_{22} - \sigma_{xy}^2) + 2n\sigma_y^2\omega_x^2 + 2n\sigma_x^2\omega_y^2 + 4n\sigma_{xy}\omega_{xy},\] \[[\Sigma_Z]_{66} = 2n\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2(2\kappa_{22}+1) - 2n\sigma_{xy}^2 + 4n\sigma_y^2\omega_x^2 + 4n\sigma_x^2\omega_y^2 + 8n\sigma_{xy}\omega_{xy}.\] Substituting and collecting terms gives the general expression \[\operatorname{Var}(\bar{S}) = \frac{1}{2n}\bigl[(2\kappa_{22}+5)\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 3\sigma_{xy}^2 + 4\sigma_{xy}\omega_{xy} + 2\sigma_y^2\omega_x^2 + 2\sigma_x^2\omega_y^2\bigr].\] Under \(H_0\) (\(\sigma_{xy} = 0\)) the last two terms vanish, yielding \[\operatorname{Var}(\bar{S}) = \frac{1}{2n}\bigl[(2\kappa_{22}+5)\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 2\sigma_y^2\omega_x^2 + 2\sigma_x^2\omega_y^2\bigr]. \qquad\square\]

Since \(V \approx \operatorname{Var}(\bar{S})\), substituting the general expression and solving gives \[\boxed{\hat\kappa_{22} = \frac{nV}{\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} - \frac{3}{2}\frac{\hat\sigma_{xy}^2}{\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} - \frac{2\hat\sigma_{xy}\hat\omega_{xy}}{\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} - \frac{\hat\omega_x^2}{\hat\sigma_x^2} - \frac{\hat\omega_y^2}{\hat\sigma_y^2} - \frac{5}{2}.}\] Under \(H_0\) (\(\hat\sigma_{xy} = 0\)) the middle two terms vanish, yielding \[\hat\kappa_{22} = \frac{nV}{\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} - \frac{\hat\omega_x^2}{\hat\sigma_x^2} - \frac{\hat\omega_y^2}{\hat\sigma_y^2} - \frac{5}{2}.\]

9.3.2 Estimating \(\kappa_{22}\) via \(V_{12}\)

An alternative estimator uses the cross-covariance \(V_{12} = \widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}(\bar S^{xx}, \bar S^{yy})\) in place of \(V_{xy}\). Since \(S_{xx} = (2T_1(X,X) - T_2(X,X))/(2n)\) and \(S_{yy} = (2T_1(Y,Y) - T_2(Y,Y))/(2n)\),

\[ \mathrm{Cov}(S_{xx}, S_{yy}) = \frac{1}{4n^2}\,\mathrm{Cov}\!\bigl(2T_1(X,X) - T_2(X,X),\; 2T_1(Y,Y) - T_2(Y,Y)\bigr). \]

Expanding by bilinearity gives a linear combination of the four cross-diagonal \(\Sigma_Z\) entries:

\[ 4\,[\Sigma_Z]_{T_1(X,X),T_1(Y,Y)} - 2\,[\Sigma_Z]_{T_1(X,X),T_2(Y,Y)} - 2\,[\Sigma_Z]_{T_2(X,X),T_1(Y,Y)} + [\Sigma_Z]_{T_2(X,X),T_2(Y,Y)}. \]

All four entries share the coefficient \(4n\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2\) on \((\kappa_{22}-1)\); the \(\omega_{xy}\) terms cancel to \(16n\sigma_{xy}\omega_{xy}\) and the \(\sigma_{xy}^2\) terms collect to \(8n\sigma_{xy}^2\). Dividing through by \(4n^2\) gives the closed form

\[ \mathrm{Cov}(S_{xx}, S_{yy}) = \frac{1}{n}\!\left[\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2(\kappa_{22}-1) + 2\sigma_{xy}^2 + 4\sigma_{xy}\omega_{xy}\right]. \]

Solving for \(\kappa_{22}\),

\[ \boxed{\hat\kappa_{22} = \frac{nV_{12} - 2\hat\sigma_{xy}^2 - 4\hat\sigma_{xy}\hat\omega_{xy}}{\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} + 1,} \]

which under \(H_0: \rho = 0\) reduces to \(\hat\kappa_{22} = nV_{12}/(\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2) + 1\), free of all \(\omega\) corrections.

9.3.3 Estimating \(\kappa_{40}\) via \(V_{xx}\)

Applying the same construction to \((X, X)\) in place of \((X, Y)\): let \(s_i^{(k)} = (X_i - X_{i+k})^2\) and \(S_i^{xx} = (s_i^{(1)} + s_{i+1}^{(1)} - s_i^{(2)})/2\), so that \(\bar S^{xx} = (2T_1(X,X) - T_2(X,X))/(2n) = \hat\sigma_x^2\). Define \[ V_{xx} := \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\bar S^{xx}) = \frac{1}{n^2}\,(\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{xx})^\top(I + C_1 + C_1^\top + C_2 + C_2^\top)\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{xx}, \qquad \tilde S_i^{xx} = S_i^{xx} - \bar S^{xx}. \]

Derivation: \(\operatorname{Var}(\bar{S}^{xx})\) from \(\Sigma_Z\)

Since \(\bar{S}^{xx} = (2T_1(X,X) - T_2(X,X))/(2n)\), \[ \operatorname{Var}(\bar{S}^{xx}) = \frac{1}{4n^2}\bigl(4[\Sigma_Z]_{11} - 4[\Sigma_Z]_{12} + [\Sigma_Z]_{22}\bigr). \] Substituting the entries from the Z-Test chapter: \[[\Sigma_Z]_{11} = 4n\kappa_{40}\sigma_x^4 + 8n\sigma_x^2\omega_x^2,\] \[[\Sigma_Z]_{12} = 4n(\kappa_{40}-1)\sigma_x^4 + 8n\sigma_x^2\omega_x^2,\] \[[\Sigma_Z]_{22} = 4n\kappa_{40}\sigma_x^4 + 16n\sigma_x^2\omega_x^2.\] Collecting terms: \[\operatorname{Var}(\bar{S}^{xx}) = \frac{1}{n}\bigl[(\kappa_{40}+4)\sigma_x^4 + 4\sigma_x^2\omega_x^2\bigr]. \qquad\square\]

Since \(V_{xx} \approx \operatorname{Var}(\bar{S}^{xx})\), substituting and solving gives \[ \boxed{\hat\kappa_{40} = \frac{nV_{xx}}{\hat\sigma_x^4} - \frac{4\hat\omega_x^2}{\hat\sigma_x^2} - 4.} \] By symmetry (\(x \leftrightarrow y\)), the same construction applied to \((Y, Y)\) yields \(\hat\kappa_{04} = nV_{yy}/\hat\sigma_y^4 - 4\hat\omega_y^2/\hat\sigma_y^2 - 4\).

9.3.4 Estimating \(\kappa_{31}\) via \(C_{xx,xy}\)

The cumulant \(\kappa_{31}\) appears in the cross-covariances between the \((X,X)\) and \((X,Y)\) blocks of \(\Sigma_Z\). Define the lag-2 cross-covariance \[ C_{xx,xy} := \frac{1}{n^2}\,(\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{xx})^\top(I + C_1 + C_1^\top + C_2 + C_2^\top)\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{xy}, \qquad \tilde S_i^{xx} = S_i^{xx} - \bar S^{xx},\quad \tilde S_i^{xy} = S_i^{xy} - \bar S^{xy}, \] which estimates \(\operatorname{Cov}(\bar S^{xx}, \bar S^{xy})\).

Derivation: \(\operatorname{Cov}(\bar{S}^{xx}, \bar{S}^{xy})\) from \(\Sigma_Z\)

Since \(\bar{S}^{xx} = (2T_1(X,X) - T_2(X,X))/(2n)\) and \(\bar{S}^{xy} = (2T_1(X,Y) - T_2(X,Y))/(2n)\), \[ \operatorname{Cov}(\bar{S}^{xx}, \bar{S}^{xy}) = \frac{1}{4n^2}\bigl(4[\Sigma_Z]_{15} - 2[\Sigma_Z]_{16} - 2[\Sigma_Z]_{25} + [\Sigma_Z]_{26}\bigr). \] The relevant entries from the Z-Test chapter are \[[\Sigma_Z]_{15} = 4n\sigma_x^3\sigma_y\kappa_{31} + 4n\sigma_{xy}\omega_x^2 + 4n\sigma_x^2\omega_{xy},\] \[[\Sigma_Z]_{16} = 4n\sigma_x^3\sigma_y\kappa_{31} - 4n\sigma_x^2\sigma_{xy} + 4n\sigma_{xy}\omega_x^2 + 4n\sigma_x^2\omega_{xy},\] \[[\Sigma_Z]_{25} = 4n\sigma_x^3\sigma_y\kappa_{31} - 4n\sigma_x^2\sigma_{xy} + 4n\sigma_{xy}\omega_x^2 + 4n\sigma_x^2\omega_{xy},\] \[[\Sigma_Z]_{26} = 4n\sigma_x^3\sigma_y\kappa_{31} + 8n\sigma_{xy}\omega_x^2 + 8n\sigma_x^2\omega_{xy}.\] Collecting terms: \[\operatorname{Cov}(\bar{S}^{xx}, \bar{S}^{xy}) = \frac{1}{n}\bigl[\sigma_x^3\sigma_y\kappa_{31} + 4\sigma_x^2\sigma_{xy} + 2\sigma_{xy}\omega_x^2 + 2\sigma_x^2\omega_{xy}\bigr]. \qquad\square\]

Since \(C_{xx,xy} \approx \operatorname{Cov}(\bar{S}^{xx}, \bar{S}^{xy})\), substituting and solving gives \[ \hat\kappa_{31} = \frac{nC_{xx,xy}}{\hat\sigma_x^3\hat\sigma_y} - \frac{4\hat\sigma_{xy}}{\hat\sigma_x\hat\sigma_y} - \frac{2\hat\sigma_{xy}\hat\omega_x^2}{\hat\sigma_x^3\hat\sigma_y} - \frac{2\hat\omega_{xy}}{\hat\sigma_x\hat\sigma_y}. \] By symmetry (\(x \leftrightarrow y\)), the same construction with \(C_{yy,xy}\) yields \(\hat\kappa_{13}\).

9.4 Estimating \(\kappa_{22}\) via Moments

Under the ECE model \(X_i = \theta_{x,i} + \varepsilon_{x,i}\), \(Y_i = \theta_{y,i} + \varepsilon_{y,i}\) with iid mean-zero errors, define \(\kappa_{22} = E[\varepsilon_{x,i}^2\varepsilon_{y,i}^2]/(\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2)\), which equals 1 under independence. Write \(T_k(\theta_x, \theta_y) = \sum_{i=1}^n (\theta_{x,i} - \theta_{x,i+k})(\theta_{y,i} - \theta_{y,i+k})\) for the signal-difference inner product and \[ \boxed{T_k^{(2)}(X, Y) = \sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - X_{i+k})^2(Y_i - Y_{i+k})^2} \] for its squared analog (applicable to any pair of sequences).

The strategy is to compute \(E[T_k^{(2)}(X, Y)]\) exactly and solve for \(\kappa_{22}\).

Lemma: \(\operatorname{Cov}\bigl((X_i - X_{i+k})^2,\,(Y_i - Y_{i+k})^2\bigr)\)

Write \(X_i - X_{i+k} = (\theta_{x,i} - \theta_{x,i+k}) + (\varepsilon_{x,i} - \varepsilon_{x,i+k})\) and \(Y_i - Y_{i+k} = (\theta_{y,i} - \theta_{y,i+k}) + (\varepsilon_{y,i} - \varepsilon_{y,i+k})\). Since the signal differences are constants, \[ \begin{aligned} &\operatorname{Cov}\bigl((X_i-X_{i+k})^2,\,(Y_i-Y_{i+k})^2\bigr) \\ &\quad= 4(\theta_{x,i}-\theta_{x,i+k})(\theta_{y,i}-\theta_{y,i+k})\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k},\,\varepsilon_{y,i}-\varepsilon_{y,i+k}) \\ &\quad\phantom{=}\; + 2(\theta_{x,i}-\theta_{x,i+k})\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k},\,(\varepsilon_{y,i}-\varepsilon_{y,i+k})^2) \\ &\quad\phantom{=}\; + 2(\theta_{y,i}-\theta_{y,i+k})\operatorname{Cov}((\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k})^2,\,\varepsilon_{y,i}-\varepsilon_{y,i+k}) \\ &\quad\phantom{=}\; + \operatorname{Cov}((\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k})^2,\,(\varepsilon_{y,i}-\varepsilon_{y,i+k})^2). \end{aligned} \] The four pieces:

  • \(\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k},\,\varepsilon_{y,i}-\varepsilon_{y,i+k}) = \sigma_{xy} + \sigma_{xy} = 2\sigma_{xy}\).

  • \(\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k},\,(\varepsilon_{y,i}-\varepsilon_{y,i+k})^2) = 0\): since \(E[\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k}] = 0\), this equals \(E[(\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k})(\varepsilon_{y,i}-\varepsilon_{y,i+k})^2]\). Swapping \(i \leftrightarrow i+k\) negates \(\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k}\) but fixes \((\varepsilon_{y,i}-\varepsilon_{y,i+k})^2\); since the pairs are iid the expectation is unchanged, so it equals its own negative and must be 0. By symmetry \(\operatorname{Cov}((\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k})^2,\,\varepsilon_{y,i}-\varepsilon_{y,i+k}) = 0\).

  • For \(\operatorname{Cov}((\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k})^2,\,(\varepsilon_{y,i}-\varepsilon_{y,i+k})^2)\): expanding using independence between time \(i\) and time \(i+k\) (and mean-zero errors), \[ E[(\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k})^2(\varepsilon_{y,i}-\varepsilon_{y,i+k})^2] = 2E[\varepsilon_{x}^2\varepsilon_{y}^2] + 2\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 4\sigma_{xy}^2 = 2\kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 2\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 4\sigma_{xy}^2, \] while \(E[(\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k})^2]E[(\varepsilon_{y,i}-\varepsilon_{y,i+k})^2] = 4\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2\), so \(\operatorname{Cov}((\varepsilon_{x,i}-\varepsilon_{x,i+k})^2,\,(\varepsilon_{y,i}-\varepsilon_{y,i+k})^2) = 2(\kappa_{22}-1)\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 4\sigma_{xy}^2\).

Combining: \[ \operatorname{Cov}\bigl((X_i-X_{i+k})^2,\,(Y_i-Y_{i+k})^2\bigr) = 8\sigma_{xy}(\theta_{x,i}-\theta_{x,i+k})(\theta_{y,i}-\theta_{y,i+k}) + 2(\kappa_{22}-1)\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 4\sigma_{xy}^2. \qquad\square \]

Lemma: \(E[T_k^{(2)}(X, Y)]\)

Exact expectation. Applying \(E[AB] = \operatorname{Cov}(A,B) + E[A]E[B]\) to each term and summing, using the lemma and \(E\!\left[\sum_i(X_i-X_{i+k})^2\right] = T_k(\theta_x,\theta_x) + 2n\sigma_x^2\): \[ \begin{aligned} E[T_k^{(2)}(X, Y)] &= 2n(\kappa_{22}-1)\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 8\sigma_{xy}T_k(\theta_x,\theta_y) + 4n\sigma_{xy}^2 \\ &\quad + T_k^{(2)}(\theta_x, \theta_y) + 2\sigma_y^2 T_k(\theta_x,\theta_x) + 2\sigma_x^2 T_k(\theta_y,\theta_y) + 4n\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2. \end{aligned} \]

Remark

As with \(2T_1(\theta_x,\theta_y) - T_2(\theta_x,\theta_y) = 0\), we have \(2T_1^{(2)}(\theta_x,\theta_y) - T_2^{(2)}(\theta_x,\theta_y) = 0\).

Linear combination. Applying the exact expectation at \(k=1\) and \(k=2\) and forming \(2E[T_1^{(2)}(X,Y)] - E[T_2^{(2)}(X,Y)]\), all \(\theta\)-dependent terms vanish by the remark and the standard property applied to \((X,X)\) and \((Y,Y)\): \[ 2E[T_1^{(2)}(X,Y)] - E[T_2^{(2)}(X,Y)] = \boxed{2n(\kappa_{22}+1)\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 4n\sigma_{xy}^2.} \]

Solving for \(\kappa_{22}\): \[ \kappa_{22} = \frac{2E[T_1^{(2)}(X,Y)] - E[T_2^{(2)}(X,Y)]}{2n\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2} - \frac{2\sigma_{xy}^2}{\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2} - 1. \]

Plugging in estimates: \[ \boxed{ \hat\kappa_{22} = \frac{2T_1^{(2)}(X,Y) - T_2^{(2)}(X,Y)}{2n\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} - \frac{2\hat\sigma_{xy}^2}{\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_y^2} - 1. } \]

9.5 Method of Moments via \(S_i\) Terms

The method-of-moments estimator for \(\kappa_{22} = E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y^2]/(\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2)\) plugs in squared residuals directly: \[\hat\kappa_{22} = \frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_i \varepsilon_{x,i}^2\,\varepsilon_{y,i}^2}{\hat\sigma_x^2\,\hat\sigma_y^2}.\] When there are no change points, \(\varepsilon_{x,i}\) and \(\varepsilon_{y,i}\) are observable as demeaned residuals, and this estimator is consistent.

Under change points, the mean \(\theta_{x,i}\) varies over time, so \(X_i - \bar X\) is contaminated by jumps and \(\varepsilon_{x,i}\) is not directly observable. We need surrogates for \(\varepsilon_{x,i}^2\) and \(\varepsilon_{y,i}^2\) that have constant expectation \(\sigma_x^2\) and \(\sigma_y^2\) at every index regardless of change-point amplitudes.

Recall from the multiplier bootstrap that \[S_i = \frac{s_i^{(1)} + s_{i+1}^{(1)} - s_i^{(2)}}{2}, \qquad s_i^{(k)} = (X_i - X_{i+k})(Y_i - Y_{i+k}),\] satisfies \(E[S_i] = \sigma_{xy}\) regardless of change-point amplitudes. Applying the same construction to each series separately gives \[S_{xx,i} = \frac{(X_i-X_{i+1})^2 + (X_{i+1}-X_{i+2})^2 - (X_i-X_{i+2})^2}{2}, \qquad S_{yy,i} = \frac{(Y_i-Y_{i+1})^2 + (Y_{i+1}-Y_{i+2})^2 - (Y_i-Y_{i+2})^2}{2},\] with \(E[S_{xx,i}] = \sigma_x^2\) and \(E[S_{yy,i}] = \sigma_y^2\) at every index, change points included. Substituting these as surrogates for \(\varepsilon_{x,i}^2\) and \(\varepsilon_{y,i}^2\) gives the ECE MoM estimator: \[\hat\kappa_{22}^{\mathrm{MoM}} = \frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_i S_{xx,i}\,S_{yy,i}}{\bar S_{xx}\,\bar S_{yy}}.\]

The \(S_i\) terms are autocorrelated at lags 1 and 2 (consecutive terms share observations), so \(\frac{1}{n}\sum S_{xx,i}S_{yy,i}\) has inflated variance relative to an i.i.d. average. The estimator is consistent for \(\kappa_{22}\) under the null \(\sigma_{xy}=0\) — the regime relevant to the independence test (proof below). For \(\sigma_{xy}\neq 0\) the surrogate product carries an \(O(\rho^2)\) bias, so \(\hat\kappa_{22}^{\mathrm{MoM}}\) targets \(\kappa_{22}+3\rho^2+\rho\,\bar B/(\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2)\) rather than \(\kappa_{22}\).

Consistency under \(\sigma_{xy}=0\): \(\frac{1}{n}\sum_i S_{xx,i}S_{yy,i} \xrightarrow{p} E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y^2]\)

Definitions. Write \(X_i - X_{i+k} = h_{x,i}^{(k)} + e_{x,i}^{(k)}\), where \(h_{x,i}^{(k)} = \theta_{x,i} - \theta_{x,i+k}\) is the signal difference and \(e_{x,i}^{(k)} = \varepsilon_{x,i} - \varepsilon_{x,i+k}\) is the noise difference. Define the squared differences \[s_{x,i}^{(k)} = \bigl(X_i - X_{i+k}\bigr)^2 = \bigl(h_{x,i}^{(k)} + e_{x,i}^{(k)}\bigr)^2,\] and likewise \(s_{y,i}^{(k)} = (Y_i - Y_{i+k})^2 = (h_{y,i}^{(k)} + e_{y,i}^{(k)})^2\). Then \[S_{xx,i} = \frac{s_{x,i}^{(1)} + s_{x,i+1}^{(1)} - s_{x,i}^{(2)}}{2}, \qquad S_{yy,i} = \frac{s_{y,i}^{(1)} + s_{y,i+1}^{(1)} - s_{y,i}^{(2)}}{2}.\]

Expanding the product. Multiplying out gives \[4\,S_{xx,i}\,S_{yy,i} = s_{x,i}^{(1)}s_{y,i}^{(1)} + s_{x,i}^{(1)}s_{y,i+1}^{(1)} - s_{x,i}^{(1)}s_{y,i}^{(2)} + s_{x,i+1}^{(1)}s_{y,i}^{(1)} + s_{x,i+1}^{(1)}s_{y,i+1}^{(1)} - s_{x,i+1}^{(1)}s_{y,i}^{(2)} - s_{x,i}^{(2)}s_{y,i}^{(1)} - s_{x,i}^{(2)}s_{y,i+1}^{(1)} + s_{x,i}^{(2)}s_{y,i}^{(2)}.\]

General term. It suffices to analyze \(E[s_{x,i}^{(k)}\,s_{y,j}^{(r)}]\) for general starting indices \(i,j\) and lags \(k,r\). Substituting the signal–noise decomposition and expanding each square gives nine terms: \[ s_{x,i}^{(k)}\,s_{y,j}^{(r)} = \begin{array}{lll} (h_{x,i}^{(k)})^2(h_{y,j}^{(r)})^2 & +\; 2(h_{x,i}^{(k)})^2 h_{y,j}^{(r)} e_{y,j}^{(r)} & +\; (h_{x,i}^{(k)})^2(e_{y,j}^{(r)})^2 \\[6pt] +\; 2h_{x,i}^{(k)} e_{x,i}^{(k)} (h_{y,j}^{(r)})^2 & +\; 4h_{x,i}^{(k)} h_{y,j}^{(r)} e_{x,i}^{(k)} e_{y,j}^{(r)} & +\; 2h_{x,i}^{(k)} e_{x,i}^{(k)}(e_{y,j}^{(r)})^2 \\[6pt] +\; (e_{x,i}^{(k)})^2(h_{y,j}^{(r)})^2 & +\; 2(e_{x,i}^{(k)})^2 h_{y,j}^{(r)} e_{y,j}^{(r)} & +\; (e_{x,i}^{(k)})^2(e_{y,j}^{(r)})^2. \end{array} \] Taking expectations and using \(E[e_{x,i}^{(k)}] = E[e_{y,j}^{(r)}] = 0\) to drop the terms in position (1,2) and (2,1) of the grid, seven terms remain: \[\begin{align} E[s_{x,i}^{(k)}\,s_{y,j}^{(r)}] &= (h_{x,i}^{(k)})^2(h_{y,j}^{(r)})^2 \\ &\quad+ (h_{x,i}^{(k)})^2\,E[(e_{y,j}^{(r)})^2] + (h_{y,j}^{(r)})^2\,E[(e_{x,i}^{(k)})^2] \\ &\quad+ 4h_{x,i}^{(k)}h_{y,j}^{(r)}\,E[e_{x,i}^{(k)}\,e_{y,j}^{(r)}] \\ &\quad+ 2h_{x,i}^{(k)}\,E[e_{x,i}^{(k)}(e_{y,j}^{(r)})^2] + 2h_{y,j}^{(r)}\,E[(e_{x,i}^{(k)})^2 e_{y,j}^{(r)}] \\ &\quad+ E[(e_{x,i}^{(k)})^2(e_{y,j}^{(r)})^2]. \end{align}\]

Case 1: \(i = j\), \(k = r\). Both increments span the same pair of time indices, so \(e_{x,i}^{(k)}\) and \(e_{y,i}^{(k)}\) involve \((\varepsilon_{x,i}, \varepsilon_{y,i})\) and \((\varepsilon_{x,i+k}, \varepsilon_{y,i+k})\). By iid: \[\begin{align} E[(e_{x,i}^{(k)})^2] &= 2\sigma_x^2, \qquad E[(e_{y,i}^{(k)})^2] = 2\sigma_y^2, \\ E[e_{x,i}^{(k)}\,e_{y,i}^{(k)}] &= 2\sigma_{xy}, \\ E[e_{x,i}^{(k)}(e_{y,i}^{(k)})^2] &= 0, \qquad E[(e_{x,i}^{(k)})^2\,e_{y,i}^{(k)}] = 0, \\ E[(e_{x,i}^{(k)})^2(e_{y,i}^{(k)})^2] &= 2\kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 2\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 4\sigma_{xy}^2. \end{align}\] Substituting into the general formula: \[E[s_{x,i}^{(k)}\,s_{y,i}^{(k)}] = (h_{x,i}^{(k)})^2(h_{y,i}^{(k)})^2 + 2\sigma_y^2(h_{x,i}^{(k)})^2 + 2\sigma_x^2(h_{y,i}^{(k)})^2 + 8\sigma_{xy}\,h_{x,i}^{(k)}\,h_{y,i}^{(k)} + 2\kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 2\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 4\sigma_{xy}^2.\]

Case 2: \(j = i+1\), \(k = r = 1\). The increments \(e_{x,i}^{(1)} = \varepsilon_{x,i} - \varepsilon_{x,i+1}\) and \(e_{y,i+1}^{(1)} = \varepsilon_{y,i+1} - \varepsilon_{y,i+2}\) share only time index \(i+1\). By iid: \[\begin{align} E[(e_{x,i}^{(1)})^2] &= 2\sigma_x^2, \qquad E[(e_{y,i+1}^{(1)})^2] = 2\sigma_y^2, \\ E[e_{x,i}^{(1)}\,e_{y,i+1}^{(1)}] &= -\sigma_{xy}, \\ E[e_{x,i}^{(1)}(e_{y,i+1}^{(1)})^2] &= -E[\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y^2], \qquad E[(e_{x,i}^{(1)})^2\,e_{y,i+1}^{(1)}] = E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y], \\ E[(e_{x,i}^{(1)})^2(e_{y,i+1}^{(1)})^2] &= \kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 3\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2. \end{align}\] Substituting into the general formula: \[\begin{align} E[s_{x,i}^{(1)}\,s_{y,i+1}^{(1)}] &= (h_{x,i}^{(1)})^2(h_{y,i+1}^{(1)})^2 + 2\sigma_y^2(h_{x,i}^{(1)})^2 + 2\sigma_x^2(h_{y,i+1}^{(1)})^2 - 4\sigma_{xy}\,h_{x,i}^{(1)}\,h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} \\ &\quad - 2h_{x,i}^{(1)}\,E[\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y^2] + 2h_{y,i+1}^{(1)}\,E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y] + \kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 3\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2. \end{align}\]

Case 3: \(i = j\), \(k = 1\), \(r = 2\). The increments \(e_{x,i}^{(1)} = \varepsilon_{x,i} - \varepsilon_{x,i+1}\) and \(e_{y,i}^{(2)} = \varepsilon_{y,i} - \varepsilon_{y,i+2}\) share only time index \(i\). By iid: \[\begin{align} E[(e_{x,i}^{(1)})^2] &= 2\sigma_x^2, \qquad E[(e_{y,i}^{(2)})^2] = 2\sigma_y^2, \\ E[e_{x,i}^{(1)}\,e_{y,i}^{(2)}] &= \sigma_{xy}, \\ E[e_{x,i}^{(1)}(e_{y,i}^{(2)})^2] &= E[\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y^2], \qquad E[(e_{x,i}^{(1)})^2\,e_{y,i}^{(2)}] = E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y], \\ E[(e_{x,i}^{(1)})^2(e_{y,i}^{(2)})^2] &= \kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 3\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2. \end{align}\] Substituting into the general formula: \[\begin{align} E[s_{x,i}^{(1)}\,s_{y,i}^{(2)}] &= (h_{x,i}^{(1)})^2(h_{y,i}^{(2)})^2 + 2\sigma_y^2(h_{x,i}^{(1)})^2 + 2\sigma_x^2(h_{y,i}^{(2)})^2 + 4\sigma_{xy}\,h_{x,i}^{(1)}\,h_{y,i}^{(2)} \\ &\quad + 2h_{x,i}^{(1)}\,E[\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y^2] + 2h_{y,i}^{(2)}\,E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y] + \kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 3\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2. \end{align}\]

Case 4: \(j = i+1\) (i.e. \(j \neq i\)), \(k = 1\), \(r = 2\). The increments \(e_{x,i+1}^{(1)} = \varepsilon_{x,i+1} - \varepsilon_{x,i+2}\) and \(e_{y,i}^{(2)} = \varepsilon_{y,i} - \varepsilon_{y,i+2}\) share only time index \(i+2\), which appears with a \(-\) sign in both. By iid: \[\begin{align} E[(e_{x,i+1}^{(1)})^2] &= 2\sigma_x^2, \qquad E[(e_{y,i}^{(2)})^2] = 2\sigma_y^2, \\ E[e_{x,i+1}^{(1)}\,e_{y,i}^{(2)}] &= \sigma_{xy}, \\ E[e_{x,i+1}^{(1)}(e_{y,i}^{(2)})^2] &= -E[\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y^2], \qquad E[(e_{x,i+1}^{(1)})^2\,e_{y,i}^{(2)}] = -E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y], \\ E[(e_{x,i+1}^{(1)})^2(e_{y,i}^{(2)})^2] &= \kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 3\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2. \end{align}\] Substituting into the general formula: \[\begin{align} E[s_{x,i+1}^{(1)}\,s_{y,i}^{(2)}] &= (h_{x,i+1}^{(1)})^2(h_{y,i}^{(2)})^2 + 2\sigma_y^2(h_{x,i+1}^{(1)})^2 + 2\sigma_x^2(h_{y,i}^{(2)})^2 + 4\sigma_{xy}\,h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}\,h_{y,i}^{(2)} \\ &\quad - 2h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}\,E[\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y^2] - 2h_{y,i}^{(2)}\,E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y] + \kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 3\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2. \end{align}\]

Assembling the sum. Substituting each case result into \(4E[S_{xx,i}S_{yy,i}]\) and grouping by term type: \[\begin{align} 4E[S_{xx,i}S_{yy,i}] &= \bigl[(h_{x,i}^{(1)})^2 + (h_{x,i+1}^{(1)})^2 - (h_{x,i}^{(2)})^2\bigr]\bigl[(h_{y,i}^{(1)})^2 + (h_{y,i+1}^{(1)})^2 - (h_{y,i}^{(2)})^2\bigr] \\ &\quad + 2\sigma_y^2\bigl[(h_{x,i}^{(1)})^2 + (h_{x,i+1}^{(1)})^2 - (h_{x,i}^{(2)})^2\bigr] + 2\sigma_x^2\bigl[(h_{y,i}^{(1)})^2 + (h_{y,i+1}^{(1)})^2 - (h_{y,i}^{(2)})^2\bigr] \\ &\quad + \sigma_{xy}\bigl[8h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(1)} - 4h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} - 4h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(2)} - 4h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(1)} + 8h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} \\ &\qquad\qquad - 4h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(2)} - 4h_{x,i}^{(2)}h_{y,i}^{(1)} - 4h_{x,i}^{(2)}h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} + 8h_{x,i}^{(2)}h_{y,i}^{(2)}\bigr] \\ &\quad + E[\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y^2]\bigl[-2h_{x,i}^{(1)} - 2h_{x,i}^{(1)} + 2h_{x,i+1}^{(1)} + 2h_{x,i+1}^{(1)} - 2h_{x,i}^{(2)} + 2h_{x,i}^{(2)}\bigr] \\ &\quad + E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y]\bigl[2h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} - 2h_{y,i}^{(2)} - 2h_{y,i}^{(1)} + 2h_{y,i}^{(2)} - 2h_{y,i}^{(1)} + 2h_{y,i+1}^{(1)}\bigr] \\ &\quad + 3(2\kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 2\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 4\sigma_{xy}^2) - 2(\kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 3\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2). \end{align}\]

Simplification. The first two lines each contain the factor \((h_{x,i}^{(1)})^2 + (h_{x,i+1}^{(1)})^2 - (h_{x,i}^{(2)})^2\). We claim \[\boxed{(h_{x,i}^{(1)})^2 + (h_{x,i+1}^{(1)})^2 - (h_{x,i}^{(2)})^2 = 0.}\] From the linear identity \(h_{x,i}^{(1)} + h_{x,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{x,i}^{(2)} = 0\) we have \(h_{x,i}^{(2)} = h_{x,i}^{(1)} + h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}\), so \[(h_{x,i}^{(1)})^2 + (h_{x,i+1}^{(1)})^2 - (h_{x,i}^{(2)})^2 = (h_{x,i}^{(1)})^2 + (h_{x,i+1}^{(1)})^2 - (h_{x,i}^{(1)} + h_{x,i+1}^{(1)})^2 = -2h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}.\] This is not yet zero, but under the minimum segment length 2 assumption consecutive lag-1 differences cannot both be nonzero, so \(h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{x,i+1}^{(1)} = 0\) at every index. The same argument applies to \(y\), so the first two lines both vanish. Collecting the remaining terms: \[\begin{align} 4E[S_{xx,i}S_{yy,i}] &= 4\sigma_{xy}\bigl[2h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(1)} - h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(2)} - h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(1)} + 2h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} \\ &\qquad\quad - h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(2)} - h_{x,i}^{(2)}h_{y,i}^{(1)} - h_{x,i}^{(2)}h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} + 2h_{x,i}^{(2)}h_{y,i}^{(2)}\bigr] \\ &\quad + 4E[\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y^2]\bigl(h_{x,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{x,i}^{(1)}\bigr) + 4E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y]\bigl(h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{y,i}^{(1)}\bigr) \\ &\quad + 4\kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 12\sigma_{xy}^2. \end{align}\] Applying the linear identities \(h_{x,i}^{(1)} + h_{x,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{x,i}^{(2)} = 0\) and \(h_{y,i}^{(1)} + h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{y,i}^{(2)} = 0\) to substitute out \(h_{x,i}^{(2)}\) and \(h_{y,i}^{(2)}\) reduces the nine-term bracket to four terms: \[\begin{align} 4E[S_{xx,i}S_{yy,i}] &= 4\sigma_{xy}\bigl[2h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(1)} - h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(1)} + 2h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}h_{y,i+1}^{(1)}\bigr] \\ &\quad + 4E[\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y^2]\bigl(h_{x,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{x,i}^{(1)}\bigr) + 4E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y]\bigl(h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{y,i}^{(1)}\bigr) \\ &\quad + 4\kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 12\sigma_{xy}^2. \end{align}\]

Mixed-lag products vanish. The two cross terms \(h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{y,i+1}^{(1)}\) and \(h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(1)}\) are identically zero at every index. Take the first: \(h_{x,i}^{(1)} = \theta_{x,i} - \theta_{x,i+1}\) is nonzero only if a change point falls in the gap \((i, i+1)\), while \(h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} = \theta_{y,i+1} - \theta_{y,i+2}\) is nonzero only if one falls in the adjacent gap \((i+1, i+2)\). Under common change-point locations, minimum segment length 2 forbids change points in adjacent gaps, so the two factors are never simultaneously nonzero and their product is \(0\). The same argument applies to \(h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(1)}\). The bracket collapses to two terms, and dividing by 4 gives \[\begin{align} E[S_{xx,i}S_{yy,i}] &= 2\sigma_{xy}\bigl(h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(1)} + h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}h_{y,i+1}^{(1)}\bigr) \\ &\quad + E[\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y^2]\bigl(h_{x,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{x,i}^{(1)}\bigr) + E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y]\bigl(h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{y,i}^{(1)}\bigr) \\ &\quad + \kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 3\sigma_{xy}^2. \end{align}\]

The signal-dependent terms make this index-varying, so \(E[S_{xx,i}S_{yy,i}] \neq \kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2\) pointwise; it is the average \(\frac{1}{n}\sum_i S_{xx,i}S_{yy,i}\) that matters. Summing over \(i\), use that \(\sum_i h_{\cdot,i+1}^{(1)} = \sum_i h_{\cdot,i}^{(1)}\), since the two are rotations of each other. The coskewness terms then cancel, \[ \sum_i \bigl(h_{x,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{x,i}^{(1)}\bigr) = 0, \] and the two covariance terms coincide, giving \[ \frac{1}{n}\sum_i E[S_{xx,i}S_{yy,i}] = \kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 3\sigma_{xy}^2 + 4\sigma_{xy}\omega_{xy}. \] Both bias terms \(3\sigma_{xy}^2\) and \(4\sigma_{xy}\omega_{xy}\) carry a factor of \(\sigma_{xy}\), so under \(H_0:\sigma_{xy}=0\) they vanish and \(\hat\kappa_{22}^{\mathrm{MoM}}\xrightarrow{p}\kappa_{22}\).

Simulation: Verifying \(\hat\kappa_{22}^{\mathrm{MoM}}\)

Since \(S_{xx,i}\) and \(S_{yy,i}\) are constructed from \((X,X)\) and \((Y,Y)\) separately, the estimator captures dependence between \(\varepsilon_x^2\) and \(\varepsilon_y^2\) without requiring \(\varepsilon_x \perp \varepsilon_y\). We verify this across three settings using scenario 2 with \(L = 4\) change points and Gaussian marginals.

  • Dependent (\(\kappa_{22} = 5\))
  • \(\lambda\)-Mixing

Scenario 2, \(L = 4\), Gaussian independent errors. Under independence \(\kappa_{22} = 1\) regardless of marginals.

Using \(\varepsilon_y = (\varepsilon_x^2 - 1)/\sqrt{2}\) with \(\varepsilon_x \sim N(0,1)\), giving \(\sigma_{xy} = 0\) and \(\kappa_{22} = 5\).

Using \(\varepsilon_y = \lambda(\varepsilon_x^2-1)/\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{1-\lambda^2}\,\eta\) with \(\varepsilon_x, \eta \stackrel{iid}{\sim} N(0,1)\), giving \(\sigma_{xy} = 0\) and \(\kappa_{22} = 1 + 4\lambda^2\).

9.5.1 Estimating \(\kappa_{40}\)

Since \(\kappa_{22}(X,X) = E[\varepsilon_x^4]/\sigma_x^4 = \kappa_{40}\), the \(\kappa_{22}\) MoM estimator applies directly with \(Y = X\). Unlike the \(\kappa_{22}\) case where \(\sigma_{xy}\) is small under the null, here \(\sigma_{xy}\vert_{Y=X} = \sigma_x^2 \neq 0\), so the bias correction is non-trivial. Substituting \(\sigma_{xy} = \sigma_x^2\) into the \(\kappa_{22}\) consistency result gives \[\frac{1}{n}\sum_i E\bigl[S_{xx,i}^2\bigr] = \kappa_{40}\sigma_x^4 + 3\sigma_x^4 + 4\sigma_x^2\omega_{xx},\] so \[\hat\kappa_{40}^{\mathrm{MoM}} = \frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_i S_{xx,i}^2 - 3\hat\sigma_x^4 - 4\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\omega_{xx}}{\hat\sigma_x^4}.\]

Consistency: \(\frac{1}{n}\sum_i S_{xx,i}^2 \xrightarrow{p} \kappa_{40}\sigma_x^4 + 3\sigma_x^4 + 4\sigma_x^2\omega_{xx}\)

Because \(\kappa_{40} = \kappa_{22}(X,X)\), we do not redo the four-case computation: we specialize the per-index master result of the \(\kappa_{22}\) consistency note to \(Y = X\). Under that substitution \(\sigma_y^2 \to \sigma_x^2\), \(\sigma_{xy} \to \sigma_x^2\), \(\kappa_{22} \to \kappa_{40}\), and both coskewnesses collapse to the skewness, \(E[\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y^2], E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y] \to \mu_3 := E[\varepsilon_x^3]\).

Pointwise mean. Setting \(Y = X\) in the per-index result \[E[S_{xx,i}S_{yy,i}] = 2\sigma_{xy}\bigl(h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(1)} + h_{x,i+1}^{(1)}h_{y,i+1}^{(1)}\bigr) + E[\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y^2]\bigl(h_{x,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{x,i}^{(1)}\bigr) + E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y]\bigl(h_{y,i+1}^{(1)} - h_{y,i}^{(1)}\bigr) + \kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 + 3\sigma_{xy}^2\] collapses the covariance bracket into a sum of squares: \[E\bigl[S_{xx,i}^2\bigr] = 2\sigma_x^2\bigl((h_i^{(1)})^2 + (h_{i+1}^{(1)})^2\bigr) + 2\mu_3\bigl(h_{i+1}^{(1)} - h_i^{(1)}\bigr) + (\kappa_{40} + 3)\sigma_x^4,\] writing \(h_i^{(k)} = \theta_{x,i} - \theta_{x,i+k}\).

Averaging over \(i\). Three pieces:

  • The skewness term telescopes. With circular indexing \(\sum_i h_{i+1}^{(1)} = \sum_i h_i^{(1)}\), so \(\sum_i 2\mu_3(h_{i+1}^{(1)} - h_i^{(1)}) = 0\).
  • The squared-jump terms each average to the signal-complexity functional \(\omega_{xx} := \frac{1}{n}\sum_i (h_i^{(1)})^2 \ge 0\) (again \(\sum_i (h_{i+1}^{(1)})^2 = \sum_i (h_i^{(1)})^2\) by rotation), contributing \(4\sigma_x^2\omega_{xx}\).
  • The constant survives as \((\kappa_{40}+3)\sigma_x^4\).

Hence \[\frac{1}{n}\sum_i E\bigl[S_{xx,i}^2\bigr] = \kappa_{40}\sigma_x^4 + 3\sigma_x^4 + 4\sigma_x^2\omega_{xx},\] which is the formula quoted above, now derived.

Why the bias does not vanish. In the \(\kappa_{22}\) case the bias \(3\sigma_{xy}^2 + 4\sigma_{xy}\omega_{xy}\) carried a factor of \(\sigma_{xy}\) and disappeared under \(H_0:\sigma_{xy}=0\). With \(Y=X\) there is no null to invoke: \(\sigma_{xy} = \sigma_x^2 \neq 0\). The \(3\sigma_x^4\) piece — arising because \(S_{xx,i}\) shares \(\varepsilon_{x,i+1}\) across its two factors — is an \(O(1)\) bias present at every sample size, while \(4\sigma_x^2\omega_{xx}\) is an \(O(L/n)\) signal bias (only the \(L\) change-point gaps contribute nonzero \(h_i^{(1)}\)). The estimator removes both explicitly through the \(-3\hat\sigma_x^4 - 4\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\omega_{xx}\) correction.

Convergence in probability. Each \(S_{xx,i}^2\) depends only on \((\varepsilon_{x,i}, \varepsilon_{x,i+1}, \varepsilon_{x,i+2})\), so the sequence is \(2\)-dependent; assuming finite eighth moments \(E[\varepsilon_x^8] < \infty\), the average has variance \(O(1/n) \to 0\), and by Chebyshev \[\frac{1}{n}\sum_i S_{xx,i}^2 \xrightarrow{p} \kappa_{40}\sigma_x^4 + 3\sigma_x^4 + 4\sigma_x^2\omega_{xx}.\] With \(\hat\sigma_x^2 \xrightarrow{p} \sigma_x^2\) and \(\hat\omega_{xx} \xrightarrow{p} \omega_{xx}\), the continuous mapping theorem gives \(\hat\kappa_{40}^{\mathrm{MoM}} \xrightarrow{p} \kappa_{40}\) — unconditionally, with no null hypothesis required.

Regression estimator and connection to quartic ECE terms

Define the quartic ECE terms \[S_{xx,i}^{(4)} = \frac{(X_i - X_{i+1})^4 + (X_{i+1} - X_{i+2})^4 - (X_i - X_{i+2})^4}{2}.\]

Write \(X_i - X_{i+k} = h_i^{(k)} + e_i^{(k)}\) where \(h_i^{(k)} = \theta_{x,i} - \theta_{x,i+k}\) and \(e_i^{(k)} = \varepsilon_{x,i} - \varepsilon_{x,i+k}\). Expanding: \[(h_i^{(k)} + e_i^{(k)})^4 = (h_i^{(k)})^4 + 4(h_i^{(k)})^3 e_i^{(k)} + 6(h_i^{(k)})^2(e_i^{(k)})^2 + 4h_i^{(k)}(e_i^{(k)})^3 + (e_i^{(k)})^4.\] Taking expectations, the odd cross-terms \(4(h_i^{(k)})^3 e_i^{(k)}\) and \(4h_i^{(k)}(e_i^{(k)})^3\) vanish: \(E[e_i^{(k)}] = 0\) trivially, and by iid (even for asymmetric distributions), \[E\bigl[(e_i^{(k)})^3\bigr] = E[\varepsilon_x^3] - E[\varepsilon_x^3] = 0.\] The remaining three groups each cancel:

Pure signal: \[(h_i^{(1)})^4 + (h_{i+1}^{(1)})^4 - (h_i^{(2)})^4 = 0\] by the change-point argument (min segment length 2 forces \(h_i^{(1)} = h_i^{(2)}\) and \(h_{i+1}^{(1)} = 0\), or vice versa).

Signal–noise cross term: \(E[6h^2 e^2] = 12\sigma_x^2(h_i^{(k)})^2\), so the combination gives \[(h_i^{(1)})^2 + (h_{i+1}^{(1)})^2 - (h_i^{(2)})^2 = 0\] by the quadratic ECE cancellation.

Pure noise: For any \(k \geq 1\), \(E[(e_i^{(k)})^4] = (2\kappa_{40}+6)\sigma_x^4\) by iid, so the combination gives \[\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[(2\kappa_{40}+6) + (2\kappa_{40}+6) - (2\kappa_{40}+6)\bigr]\sigma_x^4 = (\kappa_{40}+3)\sigma_x^4.\]

Hence \(S_{xx,i}^{(4)}\) has constant mean at every index: \[E\bigl[S_{xx,i}^{(4)}\bigr] = (\kappa_{40}+3)\sigma_x^4.\]

The residual \(+3\) arises because \(S_{xx,i}\) shares \(\varepsilon_{x,i+1}\) across its two factors. Since \(S_{xx,i}\) and \(S_{xx,i+3}\) use non-overlapping observations, \(E[S_{xx,i}\cdot S_{xx,i+3}] = \sigma_x^4\) at every index. Subtracting three times this lag-3 product gives constant mean \(\kappa_{40}\sigma_x^4\): \[\hat\kappa_{40}^{\mathrm{MoM}} = \frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_i \bigl[S_{xx,i}^{(4)} - 3\,S_{xx,i}\,S_{xx,i+3}\bigr]}{\bar S_{xx}^2}.\]

With circular indexing \(\sum_i S_{xx,i}^{(4)} = (2T_1^{(2)}(X,X) - T_2^{(2)}(X,X))/2\), so this is algebraically identical to the regression formula above when \(\tfrac{1}{n}\sum S_{xx,i}S_{xx,i+3}\) is replaced by \(\bar S_{xx}^2\).

9.5.2 Estimating \(\kappa_{31}\)

As with \(\kappa_{22}\), there are two routes to \(\kappa_{31} = E[\varepsilon_x^3\varepsilon_y]/(\sigma_x^3\sigma_y)\): a regression estimator built from a single fourth-order ECE term, and a method-of-moments estimator built from a product of two second-order ECE terms. The regression term cancels the change-point signal exactly and needs only a \(3\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_{xy}\) correction; the MoM has the lower variance (the \(\kappa_{22}\) comparison note) but its product retains change-point complexity terms that must also be subtracted.

Regression estimator. Define the cubic-linear ECE term \[T_i = \frac{t_i^{(1)} + t_{i+1}^{(1)} - t_i^{(2)}}{2}, \qquad t_i^{(k)} = (X_i - X_{i+k})^3(Y_i - Y_{i+k}).\] The change-point cancellation gives constant mean at every index, \(E[T_i] = \kappa_{31}\sigma_x^3\sigma_y + 3\sigma_x^2\sigma_{xy}\), so \[\hat\kappa_{31}^{\mathrm{reg}} = \frac{\bar T - 3\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_{xy}}{\bar S_{xx}^{3/2}\,\bar S_{yy}^{1/2}}.\]

Method of moments. Factor \(\varepsilon_x^3\varepsilon_y = \varepsilon_x^2\cdot(\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y)\) and replace each factor by its second-order ECE surrogate: \(S_{xx,i}\) for \(\varepsilon_x^2\) (mean \(\sigma_x^2\) at every index) and \(S_{xy,i}\) for \(\varepsilon_x\varepsilon_y\) (mean \(\sigma_{xy}\) at every index). Their product is a surrogate for \(\varepsilon_x^3\varepsilon_y\). Replacing the single quartic term \(T_i\) by this product lowers the variance (the \(\kappa_{22}\) comparison note), but unlike \(T_i\) — whose signal cancels exactly at every index — the product carries two change-point complexity terms, \(\omega_{xx} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i (h_{x,i}^{(1)})^2\) and \(\omega_{xy} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i h_{x,i}^{(1)}h_{y,i}^{(1)}\) (with \(h_{x,i}^{(1)} = \theta_{x,i} - \theta_{x,i+1}\)). Subtracting these alongside the \(3\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_{xy}\) bias (proof below), \[\hat\kappa_{31}^{\mathrm{MoM}} = \frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_i S_{xx,i}\,S_{xy,i} - 3\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_{xy} - 2\hat\sigma_{xy}\hat\omega_{xx} - 2\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\omega_{xy}}{\bar S_{xx}^{3/2}\,\bar S_{yy}^{1/2}},\] where \(\hat\omega_{xx}\) and \(\hat\omega_{xy}\) are the ECE complexity estimates ece.complexity(x, x) and ece.complexity(x, y).

Consistency: \(\frac{1}{n}\sum_i S_{xx,i}S_{xy,i} \xrightarrow{p} \kappa_{31}\sigma_x^3\sigma_y + 3\sigma_x^2\sigma_{xy} + 2\sigma_{xy}\omega_{xx} + 2\sigma_x^2\omega_{xy}\)

Surrogates and the general term. Split each increment into signal and noise, \(X_i - X_{i+k} = h_{x,i}^{(k)} + e_{x,i}^{(k)}\) and \(Y_i - Y_{i+k} = h_{y,i}^{(k)} + e_{y,i}^{(k)}\). With \(s_{xx,i}^{(k)} = (X_i-X_{i+k})^2\) and \(s_{xy,i}^{(k)} = (X_i-X_{i+k})(Y_i-Y_{i+k})\), \[S_{xx,i} = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(s_{xx,i}^{(1)} + s_{xx,i+1}^{(1)} - s_{xx,i}^{(2)}\bigr), \qquad S_{xy,i} = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(s_{xy,i}^{(1)} + s_{xy,i+1}^{(1)} - s_{xy,i}^{(2)}\bigr).\] The product expands into nine cross terms; it suffices to analyze the general term \(E[s_{xx,i}^{(k)}\,s_{xy,j}^{(r)}] = E[a^2 b c]\) with \(a = X_i - X_{i+k}\), \(b = X_j - X_{j+r}\), \(c = Y_j - Y_{j+r}\). Substituting the signal–noise split and using \(E[e]=0\) separates this into a pure-noise part \(E[(e_{x,i}^{(k)})^2 e_{x,j}^{(r)} e_{y,j}^{(r)}]\) plus terms each carrying at least one signal factor \(h\).

Pure-noise baseline. The pure-noise term depends only on how the index sets \(\{i,i+k\}\) and \(\{j,j+r\}\) overlap. The three diagonal combinations (matching index and lag) share both time points, where \[E\bigl[(e_x^{(k)})^3 e_y^{(k)}\bigr] = 2\kappa_{31}\sigma_x^3\sigma_y + 6\sigma_x^2\sigma_{xy};\] each of the six off-diagonal combinations shares exactly one time point and evaluates to \(\kappa_{31}\sigma_x^3\sigma_y + 3\sigma_x^2\sigma_{xy}\). Assembling with the ECE signs \((s_{xx,i}^{(1)} + s_{xx,i+1}^{(1)} - s_{xx,i}^{(2)})(s_{xy,i}^{(1)} + s_{xy,i+1}^{(1)} - s_{xy,i}^{(2)})\) — the three diagonal terms enter with \(+\), the six off-diagonal terms with net sign \(2(+) + 4(-) = -2\) — \[4\,E[S_{xx,i}S_{xy,i}]_{\text{noise}} = 3\bigl(2\kappa_{31}\sigma_x^3\sigma_y + 6\sigma_x^2\sigma_{xy}\bigr) - 2\bigl(\kappa_{31}\sigma_x^3\sigma_y + 3\sigma_x^2\sigma_{xy}\bigr) = 4\kappa_{31}\sigma_x^3\sigma_y + 12\sigma_x^2\sigma_{xy},\] so the pure-noise part has constant mean \(\kappa_{31}\sigma_x^3\sigma_y + 3\sigma_x^2\sigma_{xy}\) at every index — exactly the regression estimator’s target and bias.

Signal-dependent terms. The remaining terms each carry a signal factor \(h\). The pure-signal quartic cancels exactly (the min-segment-length-2 argument of the \(\kappa_{40}\) note), and the terms linear in a single \(h\) telescope to zero under circular rotation (as in the \(\kappa_{22}\) note). The surviving signal\(^2\times\)noise\(^2\) terms collect into two change-point complexity functionals, \[2\sigma_{xy}\,\omega_{xx} + 2\sigma_x^2\,\omega_{xy}, \qquad \omega_{xx} = \tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_i (h_{x,i}^{(1)})^2, \quad \omega_{xy} = \tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_i h_{x,i}^{(1)} h_{y,i}^{(1)}.\] Note \(\omega_{xy}\) carries no factor of \(\sigma_{xy}\), so it biases the estimator even under independence; the regression term \(T_i\), which cancels the signal pointwise, has no such terms.

Averaged mean and consistency. Collecting the pieces, \[\frac{1}{n}\sum_i E[S_{xx,i}S_{xy,i}] = \kappa_{31}\sigma_x^3\sigma_y + 3\sigma_x^2\sigma_{xy} + 2\sigma_{xy}\omega_{xx} + 2\sigma_x^2\omega_{xy}.\] Each \(S_{xx,i}S_{xy,i}\) depends only on \((\varepsilon_{\cdot,i}, \varepsilon_{\cdot,i+1}, \varepsilon_{\cdot,i+2})\), so the sequence is \(2\)-dependent; with finite eighth moments the average has variance \(O(1/n)\) and concentrates at its mean. All four bias terms are estimated consistently — \(\hat\sigma_x^2 = \bar S_{xx}\), \(\hat\sigma_{xy} = \bar S_{xy}\), and \(\hat\omega_{xx}, \hat\omega_{xy}\) by the ECE complexity regression (ece.complexity) — so subtracting them and normalizing, \[\hat\kappa_{31}^{\mathrm{MoM}} = \frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_i S_{xx,i}S_{xy,i} - 3\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\sigma_{xy} - 2\hat\sigma_{xy}\hat\omega_{xx} - 2\hat\sigma_x^2\hat\omega_{xy}}{\bar S_{xx}^{3/2}\,\bar S_{yy}^{1/2}} \xrightarrow{p} \kappa_{31}\] by the continuous mapping theorem. As with \(\kappa_{40}\), the change-point complexity terms are subtracted explicitly; the regression estimator avoids them only because its quartic term cancels the signal pointwise.

9.7 Numerical Results: Estimating \(\kappa_{22}\)

The MoM estimator has lower variance than both the regression and covariance estimators across scenarios. Between regression and covariance, neither dominates: regression wins in low-amplitude settings, covariance wins when change-point amplitude is large.

  • Regression Dominates
  • Covariance Dominates
Why does MoM have lower variance than the regression estimator?

The regression estimator computes a single fourth-order joint statistic \(T_k^{(2)} = \sum_i (X_i - X_{i+k})^2(Y_i - Y_{i+k})^2\), whereas the MoM builds separate second-order estimates \(S_{xx,i} \approx \sigma_x^2\) and \(S_{yy,i} \approx \sigma_y^2\) and multiplies them. Since \(\kappa_{22}\sigma_x^2\sigma_y^2 = E[\varepsilon_x^2\varepsilon_y^2]\) factors, this separation pays off: for independent estimates \(\hat A, \hat B\) of \(A, B\), \[\operatorname{Var}[\hat A \hat B] \approx A^2\operatorname{Var}[\hat B] + B^2\operatorname{Var}[\hat A] \leq \operatorname{Var}[\widehat{AB}].\]

Near a joint change point the advantage is even larger: \((X_i - X_{i+k})^2(Y_i - Y_{i+k})^2 \sim h_x^2 h_y^2\) is fourth-order in jump amplitudes, while \(S_{xx,i} S_{yy,i}\) is only quadratic (the signal’s squared term cancels inside each ECE term), so the regression numerator is far noisier in high-amplitude scenarios.
